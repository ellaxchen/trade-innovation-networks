What this tells us

China is the unique case. Trade and knowledge centrality rise in near lockstep from the early 2000s — the only major country where the two lines overlap throughout. The lag analysis says knowledge actually leads by 5 years (r = 0.990): Chinese innovation centrality rises first, and trade follows half a decade later. This is the opposite of the earlier app-level finding, which was an artifact of EPO's 2010 coverage discontinuity.
Japan and the US are knowledge economies. Both sit clearly above their trade lines, and the gaps are widening. Japan's trade and knowledge centrality move in opposite directions (r = −0.927) — its trade position holds while its share of global knowledge grows.
Korea is the convergence to watch. The widest trade lead in the sample (+0.236) but the fastest knowledge catch-up anywhere (trend −0.491). Canada crossed from knowledge to trade around 2009 and is still moving.
Sector-level reversal. Within sectors, China's trade leads knowledge in electronics (lag +3, r = 0.960) and machinery (lag +2, r = 0.869) — the export boom led the knowledge build-up. The country-level knowledge-first result is a composition across sectors. The US flips in pharma (knowledge leads, −5).

Method: PageRank (α = 0.85) on yearly directed graphs, weighted by trade value (BACI) and family-level citation counts (PATSTAT tls228). Chosen over eigenvector centrality because PageRank converges on directed graphs with dead-ends and one-way structure — both present here. Citations stop at 2013 (last reliable year in the spring-2018 PATSTAT edition).
